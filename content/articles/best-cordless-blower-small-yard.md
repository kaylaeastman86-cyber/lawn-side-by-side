## The short answer

If your lot is roughly a quarter acre or less — a townhome strip, a small suburban front and back, or a patio-heavy yard — you do not need a backpack blower. A handheld cordless unit around 300–450 CFM will move grass clippings, dry leaves, and driveway grit.

For most of those yards, start with the **Greenworks 40V BLF346** kit: 450 CFM, 120 MPH, and a 4.0Ah battery and charger in the box. Choose the **BLACK+DECKER BCBL700D1** when the job is decks, walks, and light debris. Choose Makita or Milwaukee if you already own that battery system. Step up to the EGO or Ryobi kit when wet, piled leaves are a regular job.

Prices below were read from the retailer page on 2026-11-09 and change often. Airflow, speed, weight, and runtime figures are manufacturer or retailer ratings, not measurements from this site.

## How to choose

1. Match CFM to the debris. About 300–450 CFM covers dry leaves and clippings on a small lot. The 650 and 730 CFM models are the ones rated for heavier, wetter piles.
2. Price the battery. A tool-only price is not the cost to start if you still need a pack and charger. The Greenworks, BLACK+DECKER, EGO, and Ryobi options above include batteries.
3. Stay on a battery system you already own. Makita 18V LXT, Milwaukee M18, Ryobi 40V, Greenworks 40V, and EGO 56V packs do not cross from one brand to another.
4. For short cleanups, published weight matters. The lighter kits are the ones built for a walk or a deck. The Ryobi listing's 9.7 lb is the heavy end of this group.
5. Use a lower speed around mulch. A dial, a low setting, or a variable trigger is what the listings describe for blowing near a bed without emptying it.

## From the field

> Blow dry leaves when you can. A soaked, matted layer needs far more air than clippings on a walk, and a dry afternoon does more than stepping up to a blower you will only use on a few wet weekends.
