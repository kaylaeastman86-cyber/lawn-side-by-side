## The short answer

For most home lawns, use a walk-behind broadcast spreader for the open grass when you put down fall fertilizer or a winterizer. It throws granules in a wide arc, so the job takes fewer passes. Use a drop spreader when beds, walks, and tight curves break the lawn up, or when you are overseeding and want seed to land only where the hopper passes.

The walk-behind models linked on this page are the Scotts Turf Builder EdgeGuard DLX and the Yard Commander YTL-140-268. On a courtyard, a parking strip, or a very small yard, a hand-crank broadcast such as the Scotts Whirl is enough. Prices change, so check the current price on each product page.

## Scotts EdgeGuard DLX

The EdgeGuard DLX is a walk-behind broadcast spreader, model 76232. Scotts calls it a rotary spreader and lists the hopper at up to 15,000 sq. ft. of Scotts product, for medium to large lawns. An impeller throws the granules out as you walk.

EdgeGuard is a lever that blocks the right side of that pattern, so you can keep the right side along a driveway, sidewalk, or bed. The spreader comes calibrated. Unfold the handle and hand-tighten the screws. Scotts lists open-spoke plastic wheels. The Amazon listing shows an item weight of 10.9 lb. The brand page describes a No-Quibble refund of the purchase price within one year.

Use the setting printed on the bag. Scotts' steps for this spreader say to fill on a hard surface, walk at a steady pace, and overlap each pass by about half, or wheel-track to wheel-track.

## Yard Commander drop

The Yard Commander YTL-140-268 is a walk-behind drop spreader. YTL International lists a 60 lb hopper, a 22-inch spread, coverage up to 15,000 sq. ft., and 12-inch never-flat tires. The brand page lists a net weight of 13.4 lb, and the Amazon listing shows the same item weight. The Amazon listing adds that how much fits depends on the material, with 60 lb as the top, and it states a 1-year limited warranty. You assemble it before the first use.

Granules fall through a gate in the bottom of the hopper, in a strip you can see. That is the pattern for a ribbon of grass between a sidewalk and a bed, and for seed you want on the soil. The owner's manual caps the load at 60 lb and says to push at a brisk walk, about 3 mph, because a faster or slower pace changes how much comes out. It also says to skip windy days when you are spreading grass seed.

## Pick the broadcast if…

- The lawn is mostly open grass and you are putting down fall fertilizer or a winterizer
- You want fewer passes on a medium or large yard
- You will use the EdgeGuard lever along the driveway and the walks

## Pick the drop if…

- Beds, curves, and narrow strips make a wide throw hard to aim
- You are overseeding and want the seed on the soil
- You would rather push a 22-inch strip and see where the last pass ended

## From the field

> Read the bag for the setting and the season window for your grass, then walk at one steady pace. Shut the flow off before you stop or turn. Overlap a broadcast pass so the pattern fills in, and on a drop spreader keep the next pass touching the last one so you do not leave a missed stripe. Fill the hopper on a hard surface so a spill stays off the lawn.
