## The short answer

Most crews moving to battery equipment do better standardizing on one platform. Every battery and charger fits every tool, so nothing sits dead on the trailer. Mixing brands only makes sense when one tool you rely on daily is clearly better elsewhere.

## Side by side

| | One platform | Mixed brands |
|---|---|---|
| Batteries | Swap between any tool | Separate sets per brand |
| Chargers | One type on the trailer | Several types |
| Tool choice | Limited to one lineup | Best tool for each job |
| Training | Simple for new hires | More to learn |
| Best for | Most crews switching to battery | Shops with one must-have tool |

## Standardize if…

- You're moving the whole crew to battery
- You charge on the trailer between stops
- You want fewer things to track and replace

## Mix brands if…

- One brand's blower or trimmer clearly outperforms for your work
- You already own a large set of batteries from two lines
- You can keep each brand's batteries clearly separated

## From the field

> Plan your battery count around your longest day, not your average day. Running out mid-route costs more than the extra battery.
