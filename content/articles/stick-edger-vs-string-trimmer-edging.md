## The short answer

If the edge is a long sidewalk, a driveway, or a curb you walk every week, buy a stick edger. The blade and the guide wheel are built to hold a straight cut at a set depth. If the edge is a short front walk, a patio, and a couple of beds, the string trimmer you already use is enough, especially when the head rotates onto a wheel.

The three tools checked for this page are the Greenworks 40V 8-inch stick edger (kit ED40L410), the ECHO PE-225 gas stick edger, and the BLACK+DECKER LSTE523, a 20V MAX string trimmer that converts to a wheeled edger. A fourth path, an edger attachment on a multi-head power head, is the EGO EA0800, and it only makes sense when you already own that power head.

Prices below were read on 2026-11-23 and change often. Specs come from the manufacturer page, the operator manual, or the retailer listing. This site did not run these tools.

## What changes along the sidewalk

**Straightness.** A stick edger rolls a wheel on the hard surface and the blade follows. That is the setup on the Greenworks and on the ECHO. The LSTE523 does the same thing only after you rotate the shaft into its wheeled edger position. A trimmer you simply tip on its side has no wheel in that position, so the straightness is whatever line you can hold by hand. On a short bed that is fine. On a long driveway the wheel is the part that keeps the cut in one place.

**Depth.** The Greenworks spec table lists a cut depth of 2.25 inches, and a FAQ on that same page says the maximum for its 8-inch edgers is up to 4 inches depending on curb height. The operator manual tells you to set the blade so it barely touches the ground in a normal stance, then tighten the knob. The ECHO manual tells you to move the wheel up for a deeper cut and down for a shallower one, and it calls 1/2 inch optimal. It does not publish a maximum. The LSTE523 listing does not publish a cut depth at all. String cuts the grass at the boundary. A blade is what cuts a groove into the soil.

**Speed.** None of these pages publish a walking speed or a minutes-per-driveway figure. What they do say is about pace. The Greenworks manual says to cut at a steady pace and to slow down if the blade bogs, and not to force the blade into the ground. The ECHO manual says to run at full throttle and to move only as fast as the blade will cut. The practical difference is the job you brought the tool out for. A stick edger is only an edger, so the whole battery or the whole tank goes to that line. The LSTE523 is also the tool you use to trim the fence and the beds, and its Amazon listing puts battery average life at 30 minutes without saying which speed or which mode that figure uses.

**Debris, and blade versus line.** A steel blade can throw soil and anything loose along the edge. Both stick edgers put a guard between that debris and you. Greenworks lists a guard and a heavy-duty trail shield. ECHO lists a high-impact nylon debris shield. The Greenworks manual requires safety glasses with side shields marked ANSI Z87.1, and it says to take the battery off before you change the depth. The ECHO manual says to pick up anything that could be thrown, to keep the edger on your right while you walk on the hard surface, and to avoid striking the concrete with the blade. It also says never to adjust the wheel with the engine running. The Greenworks manual, for its own tool, says light contact of the blade against the sidewalk edge is acceptable. Follow the manual for the tool in your hands.

Line is a different cutter, and the guard on the LSTE523 is a trimmer guard. The brand page includes that guard in the box. The ECHO manual's warning about the concrete is there because that cutter is a steel blade. A string trimmer's cutter is the line.

**Cost.** The LSTE523 was $104.94 on Amazon, sold by Amazon. The Greenworks kit was $179.98 on Amazon, sold by Amazon, and the same $179.98 on Greenworks.com, against a $299.99 list price on both. The ECHO was $319.00 on Amazon and $319.99 MSRP on ECHO's site. A stick edger sits on top of a trimmer in the budget, because the Greenworks edger does not trim weeds and the ECHO is sold as an edger. You still need something for the fence line.

**Storage.** The Greenworks and the ECHO are each a full-length tool that edges and nothing else. Greenworks says its designated edgers are not attachment-capable, so this one does not share a shaft with a trimmer. The ECHO also means a can of mixed fuel. The manual says the mix is 50:1 and tells you not to mix more than you expect to use in 30 days, or 90 days with a stabilizer. The LSTE523 is the trimmer. Edging does not add a second machine to the garage. Amazon lists its item size as 41.3 inches long by 9.2 inches wide.

**Who it fits.** A long sidewalk, a driveway, or a curb you want cut to a set depth is the stick-edger job. Pick the Greenworks kit when you want the battery and charger in the box and you would rather charge than mix fuel. Greenworks rates that kit at up to 60 minutes and up to 1/2 acre with the included 4.0Ah battery. Pick the ECHO when the edge is long enough that you would rather refuel than wait on a charger, and you are fine owning a 2-stroke. A small lot, a townhome strip, or a yard where the edge is a few minutes at the end of mowing is the trimmer job. If you already own a multi-head power head, an edger attachment is the blade-and-wheel option that does not add a second motor.

## Greenworks 40V stick edger

This is the cordless stick edger in the table. Greenworks sells the kit as product number 2701402 and model ED40L410: the edger, a 4.0Ah battery, and a 2A charger. The spec table lists 40V, a brushless motor, an 8-inch blade, a 2.25-inch cut depth, 6,500 RPM no-load, a guide wheel, a variable-speed trigger, and a heavy-duty trail shield. The operator manual lists the same no-load speed as 6,500 RPM plus or minus 10 percent, an 8-inch (203 mm) blade, and a weight of 9.59 lb without the battery. The product page rounds that weight to 9.6 lb. The Amazon listing's item weight is 11.9 lb, and it does not say whether the battery is included in that number.

The same Greenworks page says runtime is up to 60 minutes with the included 4.0Ah battery, and that the included charger takes 120 minutes. It also says the battery runs more than 75 other Greenworks 40V tools. The product page lists a 3-year limited warranty on the tool and the battery. The Amazon feature text says 4 years, so read the warranty on the page you buy from.

The manual's setup is simple: battery off, loosen the depth knob, set the blade so it barely touches the ground, tighten the knob, then check it again from a normal stance. Greenworks' FAQ says the cut is vertical, the edger is not self-propelled, and this model does not take attachments.

## ECHO PE-225 gas stick edger

ECHO lists the PE-225 as a curved-shaft gas edger with a 21.2 cc 2-stroke engine, a 7.75-inch blade that is 0.09 inches thick, a dry weight of 13.1 lb, and a 12.8 fl. oz. fuel tank. The guide wheel has captive hardware so the nut stays with the tool. A high-impact nylon shield is the debris guard. The i-30 starter is described as reducing starting effort by 30 percent. Warranty on the product page is 5 years for consumer use and 2 years commercial, and the Amazon listing's warranty line matches that.

The operator manual is where the depth and the fuel come from. It says a depth of 1/2 inch is optimal, the mix is 50:1, and you should clear the edge of anything a blade could throw. It also says to water the area first when the ground is hard, to walk on the pavement with the edger on your right, and to lower the blade at full throttle without driving it into the concrete.

The Amazon product page for this model is sold by a third-party seller. On 2026-11-23 the price was $319.00. Two fields on that same Amazon page do not match ECHO: item weight is listed as 320 lb, and cutting width as 0.09 inches. Those are not usable figures. This page uses ECHO's 13.1 lb dry weight, 7.75-inch blade, and 0.09-inch blade thickness.

### Pros

- ECHO's own page and the Amazon price were within a dollar of each other on the check date

### Cons

## Pick a stick edger if…

- The sidewalk, driveway, or curb is long enough that you want a wheel tracking it
- You want a blade that cuts a groove, at a depth you set with a knob
- You are willing to own a second tool, because an edger does not replace the trimmer
- You want the cordless kit (Greenworks, battery included) or you would rather refuel a 2-stroke (ECHO)

## Pick the string trimmer if…

- The edge is a short walk, a patio, and a few beds
- You want one machine for trimming and edging, and a place to store only that one
- You already own a trimmer and you can rotate the head or tip it so the line runs vertical
- You already own an EGO multi-head power head and would rather add the EA0800 than a whole second tool

## From the field

> Set the depth before you start, with the cutter stopped and the battery out or the engine off. The Greenworks manual says to set the blade so it barely touches the ground, then walk at a steady pace and slow down if the blade bogs. It also says a dry edge looks better, because wet soil can clog the guard. The ECHO manual says to water hard ground first so the blade can cut, and to keep the blade off the concrete. Those are two different manuals for two different tools. Read the one that came with yours, and wear safety glasses either way.
